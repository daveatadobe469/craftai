## 1. About This Guidebook

**Purpose.** This guidebook defines how Nova Fitness Collective sounds, what language is off-limits, how content must be formatted for each channel, and which legal disclosures are required before publishing. It applies to every employee, agency, freelancer, and AI tool producing customer-facing content on Nova Fitness Collective's behalf.

**Mission.** Our mission is to bring honesty and craftsmanship back to fitness, coaching small business owners through sustainable training instead of quick fixes.

**Who this is for:** Marketing, Content, Social, Customer Support, Legal & Compliance review, and any external agency or contractor producing Nova Fitness Collective content.

**Scope:** Applies to all channels -- social, email, SMS, push, web, paid media, and customer support -- across every market Nova Fitness Collective operates in.

**How to use this document:** Sections 2-3 define *how we sound*. Sections 4-6 define *what's off-limits and how content must be formatted*. Section 7 is a channel-by-channel playbook. Sections 8-9 cover legal disclosures and crisis protocol. Appendices A and B are a machine-readable rule set and worked examples, useful for training reviewers (human or automated) on real compliant vs. non-compliant content.

## 2. Brand Foundations

### Brand Story

Nova started as a single studio run by a coach who got tired of clients quitting six-week programs the moment the 'transformation' plateaued. The studio's whole philosophy -- consistency over intensity, honesty over hype -- became the voice of the app when it launched three years later.

### Positioning Statement

> For small business owners squeezing fitness into an already-full schedule, Nova is the training app that respects your time and your intelligence -- unlike programs that promise dramatic results in impossible timelines.

### Competitive Differentiation

Most fitness marketing sells transformation fantasies. Nova sells 20 realistic minutes and a coach's voice that sounds like it actually knows you skipped leg day.

### Audience Personas


**Theo, 41** -- Runs a 6-person accounting firm

- **Goals:** Fit in real training without sacrificing an evening or a Saturday.
- **Frustrations:** Programs that assume 90 minutes a day and zero work stress.
- **Primary channels:** Push notifications, app, email


**Camille, 35** -- Owns a bakery, on her feet all day

- **Goals:** Build strength without adding more standing time.
- **Frustrations:** Generic programs that ignore that she's already physically active at work.
- **Primary channels:** Instagram, in-app messaging

## 3. Messaging Pillars & Tagline Library

### Messaging Pillars


**Consistency over intensity.** We coach toward showing up, not toward unsustainable extremes.
> "20 minutes today beats 90 minutes you'll dread tomorrow."


**Respect the schedule.** Every program acknowledges the reader has a full life outside the app.
> "Built for the 20 minutes you actually have, not the 90 you wish you had."


**Honesty over hype.** We don't promise outcomes we can't back up.
> "This won't transform your life in a week. It'll make Tuesday's stairs easier by week three."

## 4. Brand Voice & Tone

Nova Fitness Collective's voice is **energetic, warm, witty, technical-but-clear**. These traits should be recognizable in every piece of content, regardless of channel or author.


### Energetic

Copy should feel like a good coach: upbeat, forward-moving, never a lecture.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "20 minutes. Real progress. Let's move." | "Users are encouraged to engage in physical activity regularly." |


### Warm

We root for people, including on the days they don't show up.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Missed yesterday's session? No lecture here -- let's just get today's rep in." | "You failed to complete your scheduled workout." |


### Witty

A little humor is welcome, especially in app copy and social -- never in injury or safety content.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Your hamstrings just filed a complaint. Stretch, please." | "WARNING: FAILURE TO STRETCH MAY RESULT IN INJURY." as a casual app tip |


### Technical-but-clear

We use real training terminology, always with a plain-language anchor.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion) -- basically, how hard did that feel, 1 to 10?" | "Target RPE: 7-8." with no explanation on first use |



### Tone by Context

| Context | Tone Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Marketing & social | Full voice expression -- lead with personality within the pillars above. |
| Transactional (receipts, confirmations) | Warm but efficient; clarity outranks personality here. |
| Customer support (routine) | Calm, helpful, human -- avoid scripted-sounding phrases. |
| Customer support (complaints or errors) | Extra empathy, zero humor, always a clear next step. |
| Legal, disclosures, and compliance copy | Neutral and precise; voice pillars do not override legal accuracy. |

## 5. Restricted & Prohibited Language

The words and phrases below are banned in all Nova Fitness Collective customer-facing content. If a word isn't listed here but feels like it violates the spirit of this list (hype, unsubstantiated claims, or off-tone language), escalate to Brand or Legal before publishing.

| Restricted Term | Category | Why It's Restricted | Use Instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| guaranteed | Unsubstantiated fitness-outcome claim | Individual fitness results depend on many factors we can't guarantee. | "designed to help you build," "many members see" |
| miracle | Unsubstantiated claim | No fitness program is a miracle; undermines the brand's honesty positioning. | "a program that actually works when you show up" |
| cheapest | Devalues coaching expertise | Positions expert coaching as a commodity. | "affordable coaching," "plans that fit your budget" |
| cheap | Brand-tone violation | Same issue as "cheapest" -- undercuts the craftsmanship positioning. | "accessible," "budget-friendly" |
| revolutionary (overused) | Diluted superlative | Overused hype word; the brand voice earns credibility through specifics, not adjectives. | "a different approach to," or name the specific mechanism |
| transform your body in X days | Unsubstantiated claim | Timelines like this are not substantiated and set unrealistic expectations. | "build noticeable strength over consistent weeks" |
| burn fat fast | Unsubstantiated / oversimplified claim | Fat loss physiology is more complex than any single program can promise. | "support a calorie deficit through consistent training" |
| no pain no gain | Brand-tone violation / safety risk | Contradicts Nova's injury-conscious, sustainable-training philosophy. | "consistent effort, not maximum pain" |
| shred / shredded (in marketing copy) | Brand-tone / unsubstantiated | Reads as hype rather than the brand's honest, coach-like tone. | describe the specific training goal instead |
| guaranteed six-pack | Unsubstantiated claim | Visible abdominal muscle depends on factors outside any program's control (body fat %, genetics). | "a program that trains your core consistently" |


### General Principles (for anything not explicitly listed)

1. **No unsubstantiated superlatives** ("best," "#1," "revolutionary") without a citable source on file with Legal.
2. **No absolute guarantees** of outcome, result, or performance -- individual results vary and must be described that way.
3. **No manufactured urgency** ("only hours left," "prices going up forever") unless the constraint is real and verifiable.
4. **No borrowed authority** (implying a certification, endorsement, or approval that hasn't been formally granted and documented).

## 6. Formatting Standards

| Standard | Rule |
|---|---|
| Capitalization | Sentence case for headlines, buttons, and subject lines (capitalize only the first word and proper nouns). Do not use Title Case or ALL CAPS except for the brand wordmark itself. |
| Oxford comma | Always used in lists of three or more items: "colors, fonts, and tone" -- not "colors, fonts and tone." |
| Numbers | Spell out one through nine in body copy; use numerals for 10 and above, all statistics, and all measurements (e.g. "3 steps," "nine reasons," "24/7"). |
| Dates | Write dates as Month D, YYYY (e.g. "July 2, 2026") in body copy; MM/DD/YYYY is permitted only in dense UI tables. |
| Emoji usage | Emoji are welcome in social and push copy (max 2 per post) to reinforce tone -- never in formal emails, legal copy, or disclosures. |
| Hashtags | Maximum 3 hashtags per social post, placed at the end of the caption, never inline in a sentence. The branded hashtag comes first. |
| Links & CTAs | Buttons and links use an active verb + outcome ("Start your plan," not "Click here" or "Learn more"). Never use a bare URL in body copy on channels that support link text. |
| Accessibility | Every image in customer-facing content must ship with descriptive alt text (not "image of..."); every video must include captions before publishing. |
| Acronyms | Spell out any acronym on first use per document/page, followed by the acronym in parentheses; the acronym alone may be used thereafter. |
| Contractions | Contractions are allowed and encouraged ("we're," "you'll") to keep the voice conversational, except inside formal disclosures and legal text. |

- Currency, date, and measurement formats follow local convention (e.g. DD/MM/YYYY and metric units outside the US) even when the surrounding copy is in English for a US-based CMS.

## 7. Channel Playbook

Character limits are hard constraints (Section 6 covers the full table); this section covers *how* to use each channel well within those constraints.

### Instagram

Lead visually; caption supports the image rather than repeating it. Best for brand-world content, product detail, and community-facing tone.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Hey — meet the Nova Fitness Collective way to spend less time worrying about the details. Nova — Do It Right. #Nova

### TikTok

Fastest-moving channel -- voice can flex slightly more casual here than elsewhere, but restricted words and disclosures still apply in full.

**Example (on-brand):**
> (see brand voice section for tone reference)

### LinkedIn

Most formal social channel. Use for company milestones, hiring, thought leadership -- tone shifts toward "sophisticated" and "reassuring" pillars over "witty" or "edgy" ones.

**Example (on-brand):**
> (see brand voice section for tone reference)

### Email

Highest-trust, most personal channel. Subject line earns the open; body copy earns the click. Disclosures are never optional here.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Okay, this is exciting — how Nova Fitness Collective helps you spend less time worrying about the details

### SMS / Push

Shortest attention span, highest interruption cost. One idea per message, always.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Quick one for you! Your Nova Fitness Collective update is ready — a quick way to spend less time worrying about the details.

### Blog / SEO

Longest-form brand voice expression. This is where a voice pillar can be explained, not just demonstrated.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Nova Fitness Collective today reaffirmed its mission to bring honesty and craftsmanship back to fitness & wellness, announcing a new initiative aimed at helping Small Business Owners spend less time worrying about the details.

### Paid Search / Display

Least room for personality -- clarity and compliance outrank cleverness. Every claim needs to survive legal review at 30 characters.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Nova Fitness Collective: Nova — Do It Right.

### Customer Support

Voice pillars still apply, but empathy and clarity always outrank wit or edge, especially in complaints or errors.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Thanks for reaching out! We hear you, and we're on it — Nova Fitness Collective's here to make sure you spend less time worrying about the details. We'll follow up within one business day.



### Channel-Specific Character Limits

| Channel | Field | Character Limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| X / Twitter | Post text | 280 characters | Leave room for a link (~23 chars) if sharing one. |
| Instagram | Caption | 2,200 characters (aim for first 125 visible) | Front-load the hook; caption truncates after ~3 lines. |
| Instagram | Bio | 150 characters | One line of value prop + one line of CTA max. |
| Facebook | Post text | 63,206 characters (aim for under 80 for engagement) | Short posts consistently outperform long ones in feed. |
| LinkedIn | Post text | 3,000 characters (aim for under 200 above the fold) | First 2-3 lines show before 'See more' -- put the point there. |
| TikTok | Caption | 2,200 characters (aim for under 150) | Hook must land in the first 3 words. |
| Email | Subject line | 60 characters (front-load first 30 for mobile) | Avoid ALL CAPS and more than one exclamation point. |
| Email | Preview text | 90 characters | Must not repeat the subject line verbatim. |
| SMS / Text | Message body | 160 characters (single segment) | Longer messages split into multiple segments and may be billed/delivered separately. |
| Push Notification | Title | 50 characters | Titles longer than this truncate on most Android/iOS lock screens. |
| Push Notification | Body | 150 characters | Lead with the benefit, not the feature. |
| SEO / Meta | Meta description | 155-160 characters | Google truncates beyond this; include the primary keyword early. |
| Paid Search | Headline (Google Ads) | 30 characters per headline | Up to 3 headlines are combined; each must stand alone. |
| Blog / Web | Page title / H1 | 60 characters | Keep the brand name out of the title unless it's the subject. |

## 8. Required Disclosures & Disclaimers

Nova Fitness Collective operates in a category with specific disclosure obligations. The table below defines exactly when a disclosure is required, the approved wording, and where it must appear. Do not paraphrase disclosure text without Legal sign-off.

| Content Scenario | Required Disclosure Text | Placement Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Any content describing a physical outcome (weight, strength, endurance change) | "Results may vary based on individual effort, starting point, and consistency." | Directly below the claim or testimonial. |
| Member transformation posts | "[Name] followed a personalized program; individual results are not guaranteed." | In the caption, not only in a linked bio. |


**General disclaimer (use when no scenario above applies but a claim is being made):**
> "Results may vary based on individual effort, starting point, and consistency."

### Regional Disclosure Requirements

| Region | Requirement |
|---|---|
| United States | FTC guidance on fitness testimonials applies: include a disclaimer that results are not typical unless substantiated by data. |
| General / all markets | Any program change or new movement should include: "Consult a physician before beginning any new exercise program." |

## 9. Crisis & Sensitive-Topic Communication Protocol

**Acknowledge fast.** Publicly acknowledge any service disruption, error, or complaint pattern within 1 hour, even if the full fix isn't ready. Silence reads worse than an honest "we're on it."

**Drop the personality, keep the humanity.** Suspend Nova Fitness Collective's lighter tone pillars (wit, edge, playfulness) during an active incident. Empathy and clarity remain non-negotiable.

**One voice, one channel of record.** Designate a single channel (usually the status page or a pinned post) as the source of truth; all other channels link back to it rather than repeating varying details.

**No speculation.** Never guess at root cause, timeline, or blame publicly. State only what is confirmed.

**Close the loop.** Once resolved, publish a plain-language summary of what happened and what changed -- silence after resolution erodes trust as much as silence during the incident.

**Legal & Compliance sign-off.** Any incident involving data, safety, or regulated claims requires Compliance sign-off before any public statement, even a holding statement.

## Appendix A: Machine-Readable Rule Reference

The rules below are the atomic, structured version of Sections 4-8, formatted for ingestion by content-review tooling (human or AI). Each rule maps to a Rule ID for traceability.

| Rule ID | Category | Rule Text |
|---|---|---|
| RL0001 | Logo | The Nova Fitness Collective logo may only appear on backgrounds that meet a minimum 4.5:1 contrast ratio against #22333B. |
| RL0002 | Logo | The Nova Fitness Collective logo must always maintain clear space equal to the width of the icon on all sides; no text, imagery, or UI elements may intrude on this zone. |
| RL0003 | Color | #C6AC8F is reserved for calls-to-action and small highlight moments — it must never be used as a large background field. |
| RL0004 | Color | #22333B is the dominant brand color and must anchor at least 60% of any Nova Fitness Collective visual composition; #EAE0D5 and #C6AC8F are supporting colors only. |
| RL0005 | Typography | Space Grotesk is used exclusively for headlines and display copy; Work Sans is used for all body text and UI copy. |
| RL0006 | Typography | Never substitute a system font for Space Grotesk or Work Sans in customer-facing materials without design team sign-off. |
| RL0007 | Voice & Tone | Nova Fitness Collective's voice is energetic, warm, witty, technical-but-clear. Copy that reads as the opposite of these traits (e.g. cold, generic, or overly formal where warmth is expected) is off-brand. |
| RL0008 | Voice & Tone | Every piece of Nova Fitness Collective copy should sound like it could only come from Nova Fitness Collective — avoid generic category language that any competitor could use verbatim. |
| RL0009 | Messaging | Nova Fitness Collective never disparages named competitors by name in public-facing content. |
| RL0010 | Messaging | All Nova Fitness Collective messaging must tie back to the mission: Our mission is to bring honesty and craftsmanship back to fitness & wellness. |
| RL0011 | Legal & Compliance | Content that makes claims requiring substantiation must include the disclaimer: 'N/A — no regulated claims category' |
| RL0012 | Imagery | Nova Fitness Collective imagery style is: bright, natural light with candid human moments — no stock-photo stiffness. Stock photography that contradicts this direction should be rejected in creative review. |
