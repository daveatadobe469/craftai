## 1. About This Guidebook

**Purpose.** This guidebook defines how Pathforge Fintech sounds, what language is off-limits, how content must be formatted for each channel, and which legal disclosures are required before publishing. It applies to every employee, agency, freelancer, and AI tool producing customer-facing content on Pathforge Fintech's behalf.

**Mission.** Our mission is to make personal finance feel approachable instead of intimidating for people opening their first account.

**Who this is for:** Marketing, Content, Social, Customer Support, Legal & Compliance review, and any external agency or contractor producing Pathforge Fintech content.

**Scope:** Applies to all channels -- social, email, SMS, push, web, paid media, and customer support -- across every market Pathforge Fintech operates in.

**How to use this document:** Sections 2-3 define *how we sound*. Sections 4-6 define *what's off-limits and how content must be formatted*. Section 7 is a channel-by-channel playbook. Sections 8-9 cover legal disclosures and crisis protocol. Appendices A and B are a machine-readable rule set and worked examples, useful for training reviewers (human or automated) on real compliant vs. non-compliant content.

## 2. Brand Foundations

### Brand Story

Pathforge was founded after its co-founders watched their own parents get quoted three different answers for the same loan question. The company's first internal doc wasn't a business plan -- it was a glossary translating bank jargon into plain English. That glossary became the seed of this guidebook.

### Positioning Statement

> For people opening their first real bank or investment account, Pathforge is the neobank that explains every fee and feature in plain language -- unlike traditional banks that bury the details in a 40-page disclosure PDF.

### Competitive Differentiation

Legacy banks lead with product features and bury the fine print. Pathforge leads with the fine print, in plain English, right where the decision is being made -- then gets out of the way.

### Audience Personas


**Riley, 23** -- First job out of college, first bank account outside a student account

- **Goals:** Understand what's normal to pay in fees and what isn't.
- **Frustrations:** Being upsold products she doesn't understand, hidden minimums.
- **Primary channels:** In-app, push, social (Instagram/TikTok)


**The Osei family, small business owners** -- Running a two-person landscaping business

- **Goals:** Separate business and personal finances without hiring a bookkeeper.
- **Frustrations:** Business banking products written for enterprises, not solo operators.
- **Primary channels:** Email, web, phone support

## 3. Messaging Pillars & Tagline Library

### Messaging Pillars


**Fees in plain sight.** Every fee is named, explained, and shown before the moment of commitment.
> "$0 monthly fee. $3 for out-of-network ATMs, refunded up to 3x/month."


**No first-account shame.** We never assume the reader already knows how banking works.
> "New to investing? Here's the 60-second version."


**Confidence through math, not hype.** We show the actual number instead of a vague promise.
> "That's an extra $340 a year, on average, for accounts like yours."

## 4. Brand Voice & Tone

Pathforge Fintech's voice is **reassuring, sophisticated, inclusive, playful**. These traits should be recognizable in every piece of content, regardless of channel or author.


### Reassuring

Money is emotional. We lead with stability before we lead with features.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Your money is FDIC-insured and always accessible -- here's how the fee works." | "Fee schedule: see Section 4.2." |


### Sophisticated

We respect the reader's intelligence; we simplify without dumbing down.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Compound interest means your interest also earns interest -- here's your number." | "It's basically magic money math!" |


### Inclusive

We never assume prior financial knowledge or a specific financial background.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "No credit history? You can still build one with us -- here's how." | "Obviously you'll want to check your credit score first." |


### Playful (in restraint)

Light warmth in onboarding and app copy; never in anything involving money movement or risk.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Nice! Your first paycheck just landed." (in-app, low stakes) | "Oops! Your transfer might have failed, who knows!" (money-movement error) |



### Tone by Context

| Context | Tone Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Marketing & social | Full voice expression -- lead with personality within the pillars above. |
| Transactional (receipts, confirmations) | Warm but efficient; clarity outranks personality here. |
| Customer support (routine) | Calm, helpful, human -- avoid scripted-sounding phrases. |
| Customer support (complaints or errors) | Extra empathy, zero humor, always a clear next step. |
| Legal, disclosures, and compliance copy | Neutral and precise; voice pillars do not override legal accuracy. |

## 5. Restricted & Prohibited Language

The words and phrases below are banned in all Pathforge Fintech customer-facing content. If a word isn't listed here but feels like it violates the spirit of this list (hype, unsubstantiated claims, or off-tone language), escalate to Brand or Legal before publishing.

| Restricted Term | Category | Why It's Restricted | Use Instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| guaranteed returns | Regulatory risk (SEC/FINRA) | No investment return can be guaranteed; this is a securities-law red line. | "historical average return of X% (past performance does not guarantee future results)" |
| no risk | Regulatory risk | All investment and most financial products carry some risk. | "lower-risk option," "FDIC-insured up to $250,000" |
| get rich | Unsubstantiated / predatory framing | Implies unrealistic outcomes and can read as predatory marketing. | "grow your savings," "build long-term wealth" |
| revolutionary (overused) | Brand-tone / credibility risk | Overused superlative that reads as hype rather than substance. | "a simpler way to," "built differently" |
| miracle | Unsubstantiated claim | Financial products are not miraculous; this invites regulatory scrutiny. | "a smarter default," "an easier starting point" |
| beat the market | Regulatory risk (SEC/FINRA) | Implies guaranteed outperformance, a securities-marketing red line. | "track an index designed to," with historical data cited |
| insider tip | Regulatory / legal risk | Implies non-public information, which is both misleading and potentially illegal to imply. | "a strategy many investors use" |
| can't lose | Regulatory risk | All investing carries risk of loss; this phrase alone can trigger regulatory action. | "a lower-volatility option" |
| free money (re: bonuses/APY) | Misleading framing | Bonuses and APY have terms and are not literally free. | "$50 bonus after your first deposit -- terms apply" |
| passive income guaranteed | Regulatory risk | Combines two red-flag terms in one phrase. | "a way to put your savings to work" |
| debt-free fast | Unsubstantiated / predatory framing | Implies a universal timeline that doesn't exist. | "a plan to pay down debt faster", with a real example timeline |
| pre-approved (unless verified) | Legal / TILA risk | Only use if the specific offer meets legal pre-approval criteria. | "check your rate in 60 seconds, no credit impact" |


### General Principles (for anything not explicitly listed)

1. **No unsubstantiated superlatives** ("best," "#1," "revolutionary") without a citable source on file with Legal.
2. **No absolute guarantees** of outcome, result, or performance -- individual results vary and must be described that way.
3. **No manufactured urgency** ("only hours left," "prices going up forever") unless the constraint is real and verifiable.
4. **No borrowed authority** (implying a certification, endorsement, or approval that hasn't been formally granted and documented).

## 6. Formatting Standards

| Standard | Rule |
|---|---|
| Capitalization | Sentence case for headlines, buttons, and subject lines (capitalize only the first word and proper nouns). Do not use Title Case or ALL CAPS except for the brand wordmark itself. |
| Oxford comma | Always used in lists of three or more items: "colors, fonts, and tone" -- not "colors, fonts and tone." |
| Numbers | Spell out one through nine in body copy; use numerals for 10 and above, all statistics, and all measurements (e.g. "3 steps," "nine reasons," "24/7"). |
| Dates | Write dates as Month D, YYYY (e.g. "July 2, 2026") in body copy; MM/DD/YYYY is permitted only in dense UI tables. |
| Emoji usage | Emoji are welcome in social and push copy (max 2 per post) to reinforce tone -- never in formal emails, legal copy, or disclosures. |
| Hashtags | Maximum 3 hashtags per social post, placed at the end of the caption, never inline in a sentence. The branded hashtag comes first. |
| Links & CTAs | Buttons and links use an active verb + outcome ("Start your plan," not "Click here" or "Learn more"). Never use a bare URL in body copy on channels that support link text. |
| Accessibility | Every image in customer-facing content must ship with descriptive alt text (not "image of..."); every video must include captions before publishing. |
| Acronyms | Spell out any acronym on first use per document/page, followed by the acronym in parentheses; the acronym alone may be used thereafter. |
| Contractions | Contractions are allowed and encouraged ("we're," "you'll") to keep the voice conversational, except inside formal disclosures and legal text. |

### Localization Notes

- Translated content must be reviewed by a native-speaking editor, not machine-translated and published directly.
- Idioms and wordplay in the Clash Display-styled headline system rarely translate -- prioritize meaning over literal wording when localizing.
- Currency, date, and measurement formats follow local convention (e.g. DD/MM/YYYY and metric units outside the US) even when the surrounding copy is in English for a US-based CMS.

## 7. Channel Playbook

Character limits are hard constraints (Section 6 covers the full table); this section covers *how* to use each channel well within those constraints.

### Instagram

Lead visually; caption supports the image rather than repeating it. Best for brand-world content, product detail, and community-facing tone.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Let's be honest — meet the Pathforge Fintech way to get more done with less hassle. Pathforge. Made Simple. #Pathforge

### TikTok

Fastest-moving channel -- voice can flex slightly more casual here than elsewhere, but restricted words and disclosures still apply in full.

**Example (on-brand):**
> VOICEOVER: 'At Pathforge Fintech, we believe First-Time Buyers deserve better. That's why we help you get more done with less hassle.'

### LinkedIn

Most formal social channel. Use for company milestones, hiring, thought leadership -- tone shifts toward "sophisticated" and "reassuring" pillars over "witty" or "edgy" ones.

**Example (on-brand):**
> (see brand voice section for tone reference)

### Email

Highest-trust, most personal channel. Subject line earns the open; body copy earns the click. Disclosures are never optional here.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Okay, this is exciting — how Pathforge Fintech helps you get more done with less hassle

### SMS / Push

Shortest attention span, highest interruption cost. One idea per message, always.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Let's be honest! Your Pathforge Fintech update is ready — a quick way to get more done with less hassle.

### Blog / SEO

Longest-form brand voice expression. This is where a voice pillar can be explained, not just demonstrated.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Pathforge. Made Simple. Pathforge Fintech exists so First-Time Buyers can get more done with less hassle — nothing more complicated than that.

### Paid Search / Display

Least room for personality -- clarity and compliance outrank cleverness. Every claim needs to survive legal review at 30 characters.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Pathforge Fintech: Pathforge. Made Simple.

### Customer Support

Voice pillars still apply, but empathy and clarity always outrank wit or edge, especially in complaints or errors.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Thanks for reaching out! We hear you, and we're on it — Pathforge Fintech's here to make sure you get more done with less hassle. We'll follow up within one business day.



### Channel-Specific Character Limits

| Channel | Field | Character Limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| X / Twitter | Post text | 280 characters | Leave room for a link (~23 chars) if sharing one. |
| Instagram | Caption | 2,200 characters (aim for first 125 visible) | Front-load the hook; caption truncates after ~3 lines. |
| Instagram | Bio | 150 characters | One line of value prop + one line of CTA max. |
| Facebook | Post text | 63,206 characters (aim for under 80 for engagement) | Short posts consistently outperform long ones in feed. |
| LinkedIn | Post text | 3,000 characters (aim for under 200 above the fold) | First 2-3 lines show before 'See more' -- put the point there. |
| TikTok | Caption | 2,200 characters (aim for under 150) | Hook must land in the first 3 words. |
| Email | Subject line | 60 characters (front-load first 30 for mobile) | Avoid ALL CAPS and more than one exclamation point. |
| Email | Preview text | 90 characters | Must not repeat the subject line verbatim. |
| SMS / Text | Message body | 160 characters (single segment) | Longer messages split into multiple segments and may be billed/delivered separately. |
| Push Notification | Title | 50 characters | Titles longer than this truncate on most Android/iOS lock screens. |
| Push Notification | Body | 150 characters | Lead with the benefit, not the feature. |
| SEO / Meta | Meta description | 155-160 characters | Google truncates beyond this; include the primary keyword early. |
| Paid Search | Headline (Google Ads) | 30 characters per headline | Up to 3 headlines are combined; each must stand alone. |
| Blog / Web | Page title / H1 | 60 characters | Keep the brand name out of the title unless it's the subject. |

## 8. Required Disclosures & Disclaimers

Pathforge Fintech operates in a category with specific disclosure obligations. The table below defines exactly when a disclosure is required, the approved wording, and where it must appear. Do not paraphrase disclosure text without Legal sign-off.

| Content Scenario | Required Disclosure Text | Placement Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Any mention of investment growth, returns, or performance | "Past performance does not guarantee future results. All investing involves risk, including loss of principal." | Directly below the performance statement, not in a linked footnote only. |
| Any mention of FDIC/deposit insurance | "Deposits are FDIC-insured up to $250,000 per depositor, per ownership category." | Same paragraph as the insurance claim. |
| Referral or affiliate promotions | "Pathforge may receive compensation for this referral. Terms and conditions apply; see website for full details." | Before the CTA button, visible without scrolling on mobile. |
| APR, interest rate, or fee mentions in ads | "Rates and terms subject to change; see current rates at pathforge.com/rates." | Immediately adjacent to the rate/fee figure. |


**General disclaimer (use when no scenario above applies but a claim is being made):**
> "Past performance does not guarantee future results. All investing involves risk, including loss of principal."

### Regional Disclosure Requirements

| Region | Requirement |
|---|---|
| United States | Regulation E notice required for electronic transfers: "Your rights and liabilities are described in our Electronic Fund Transfer disclosure." |
| European Union | PSD2 notice required for payment services: "Payment services are provided in accordance with PSD2 and applicable EU consumer protection law." |
| United Kingdom | FCA notice required: "Pathforge UK is authorised and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority." |

## 9. Crisis & Sensitive-Topic Communication Protocol

**Acknowledge fast.** Publicly acknowledge any service disruption, error, or complaint pattern within 1 hour, even if the full fix isn't ready. Silence reads worse than an honest "we're on it."

**Drop the personality, keep the humanity.** Suspend Pathforge Fintech's lighter tone pillars (wit, edge, playfulness) during an active incident. Empathy and clarity remain non-negotiable.

**One voice, one channel of record.** Designate a single channel (usually the status page or a pinned post) as the source of truth; all other channels link back to it rather than repeating varying details.

**No speculation.** Never guess at root cause, timeline, or blame publicly. State only what is confirmed.

**Close the loop.** Once resolved, publish a plain-language summary of what happened and what changed -- silence after resolution erodes trust as much as silence during the incident.

**Legal & Compliance sign-off.** Any incident involving data, safety, or regulated claims requires Compliance sign-off before any public statement, even a holding statement.

## Appendix A: Machine-Readable Rule Reference

The rules below are the atomic, structured version of Sections 4-8, formatted for ingestion by content-review tooling (human or AI). Each rule maps to a Rule ID for traceability.

| Rule ID | Category | Rule Text |
|---|---|---|
| RL0337 | Logo | The Pathforge Fintech logo must always maintain clear space equal to 2x the logo's negative-space padding on all sides; no text, imagery, or UI elements may intrude on this zone. |
| RL0338 | Logo | Never display the Pathforge Fintech logo smaller than 20px digital / 0.4in print; below this size the wordmark becomes illegible. |
| RL0339 | Color | #6FFFE9 is reserved for calls-to-action and small highlight moments — it must never be used as a large background field. |
| RL0340 | Color | Never substitute unapproved colors for #0B132B, #5BC0BE, or #6FFFE9 in any customer-facing material. |
| RL0341 | Typography | Headline text set in Clash Display must never fall below 18pt in digital contexts for accessibility. |
| RL0342 | Typography | Never substitute a system font for Clash Display or Satoshi in customer-facing materials without design team sign-off. |
| RL0343 | Voice & Tone | Pathforge Fintech's voice is reassuring, sophisticated, inclusive, playful. Copy that reads as the opposite of these traits (e.g. cold, generic, or overly formal where warmth is expected) is off-brand. |
| RL0344 | Voice & Tone | Pathforge Fintech never talks down to First-Time Buyers; explain, don't lecture. |
