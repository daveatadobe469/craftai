## 1. About This Guidebook

**Purpose.** This guidebook defines how Auravue Beauty sounds, what language is off-limits, how content must be formatted for each channel, and which legal disclosures are required before publishing. It applies to every employee, agency, freelancer, and AI tool producing customer-facing content on Auravue Beauty's behalf.

**Mission.** Our mission is to challenge beauty-industry hype with straight-talking, ingredient-honest products for a generation done with filters.

**Who this is for:** Marketing, Content, Social, Customer Support, Legal & Compliance review, and any external agency or contractor producing Auravue Beauty content.

**Scope:** Applies to all channels -- social, email, SMS, push, web, paid media, and customer support -- across every market Auravue Beauty operates in.

**How to use this document:** Sections 2-3 define *how we sound*. Sections 4-6 define *what's off-limits and how content must be formatted*. Section 7 is a channel-by-channel playbook. Sections 8-9 cover legal disclosures and crisis protocol. Appendices A and B are a machine-readable rule set and worked examples, useful for training reviewers (human or automated) on real compliant vs. non-compliant content.

## 2. Brand Foundations

### Brand Story

Auravue's founder walked out of a dermatology internship fed up with 'clean beauty' claims that had no legal definition and no ingredient list to back them up. The brand's first product shipped with the full ingredient percentage breakdown printed on the box -- a habit that became policy.

### Positioning Statement

> For Gen Z shoppers who read the ingredient list before the marketing copy, Auravue is the beauty brand that shows its work -- unlike brands that lean on vague 'clean' and 'natural' labels with nothing to substantiate them.

### Competitive Differentiation

Most beauty marketing sells a feeling. Auravue sells the feeling *and* shows the percentage of active ingredient that earns it -- confidence backed by a spec sheet, not a mood board.

### Audience Personas


**Jae, 19** -- College student, skincare-forum lurker

- **Goals:** Find products that actually match what influencers claim, verified by ingredients.
- **Frustrations:** Vague marketing claims, brands that don't list percentages.
- **Primary channels:** TikTok, Instagram, Reddit-adjacent research habits


**Priya, 26** -- Sensitive skin, burned by 'natural' products before

- **Goals:** Trust that a claim on the label is actually true.
- **Frustrations:** Greenwashing, brands that hide behind 'proprietary blend.'
- **Primary channels:** Instagram, email, product review sites

## 3. Messaging Pillars & Tagline Library

### Messaging Pillars


**Ingredients over adjectives.** We name the percentage, not just the promise.
> "10% niacinamide. That's the whole formula story."


**Confidence, not hype.** We take a stance without resorting to superlatives we can't back up.
> "This isn't the 'best serum ever.' It's 10% niacinamide that does what 10% niacinamide does."


**Calling out industry nonsense.** We name vague industry terms and explain what they actually mean (or don't).
> "'Dermatologist tested' doesn't mean 'dermatologist approved.' Here's the difference."

## 4. Brand Voice & Tone

Auravue Beauty's voice is **edgy, energetic, bold, authoritative**. These traits should be recognizable in every piece of content, regardless of channel or author.


### Edgy

We're not afraid of an opinion, including calling out industry nonsense.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "'Clean beauty' is a marketing term, not a legal one. Here's what's actually in this." | "Our products are 100% clean and pure!" (undefined claim) |


### Energetic

Short sentences. Real momentum. No corporate throat-clearing.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "New drop. Zero fillers. Let's go." | "We are pleased to announce the availability of our newest product line." |


### Bold

We take a stance instead of hedging every claim into mush.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "This is the only serum in your routine that actually needs a patch test. Do it." | "Some users may potentially experience some effects." |


### Authoritative

Confidence is backed by ingredient transparency, not vague superiority.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "2% salicylic acid. That's the whole story." | "Our secret formula outperforms everything else." |



### Tone by Context

| Context | Tone Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Marketing & social | Full voice expression -- lead with personality within the pillars above. |
| Transactional (receipts, confirmations) | Warm but efficient; clarity outranks personality here. |
| Customer support (routine) | Calm, helpful, human -- avoid scripted-sounding phrases. |
| Customer support (complaints or errors) | Extra empathy, zero humor, always a clear next step. |
| Legal, disclosures, and compliance copy | Neutral and precise; voice pillars do not override legal accuracy. |

## 5. Restricted & Prohibited Language

The words and phrases below are banned in all Auravue Beauty customer-facing content. If a word isn't listed here but feels like it violates the spirit of this list (hype, unsubstantiated claims, or off-tone language), escalate to Brand or Legal before publishing.

| Restricted Term | Category | Why It's Restricted | Use Instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| insane deal | Brand-tone / hype | Reads as discount-bin hype, contradicts the brand's confident-not-desperate tone. | "limited restock," "back in stock" |
| cheapest | Devalues the product | Positions the brand on price rather than formulation integrity. | "accessible price point" |
| risk-free | Regulatory / FTC risk | Cosmetic claims implying zero risk of reaction are not substantiable. | "patch-test recommended," "gentle formula" |
| best in the world | Unsubstantiated superlative | Not a claim we can substantiate and inconsistent with the ingredient-transparency positioning. | cite the specific % active ingredient instead |
| hack | Overused / diluted term | Diluted internet slang that reads as try-hard rather than authoritative. | "shortcut," "the one-step version" |
| clinically proven (without citation) | FTC substantiation risk | Any efficacy claim requires a citable study on file with legal. | "in a consumer study of 100 users..." with the study linked |
| dermatologist approved (unqualified) | FTC substantiation risk | Requires a named study or board; 'dermatologist tested' and 'approved' are not interchangeable. | "reviewed by our dermatology advisory panel" (if true and documented) |
| chemical-free | Scientifically meaningless / misleading | Everything, including water, is a chemical; this phrase misleads on safety. | "free from [specific named ingredient]" |
| anti-aging (unqualified) | FTC substantiation risk | Implies a health claim; must be tied to a specific, substantiated mechanism. | "supports skin elasticity" with cited data |
| guaranteed to work for everyone | Unsubstantiated claim | Skin responses vary; blanket guarantees invite complaints. | "formulated for most skin types; patch test recommended" |
| toxin-free | Scientifically vague / misleading | "Toxin" is not a defined regulatory term in this context. | name the specific excluded ingredient (e.g. "sulfate-free") |
| cruelty-free (unless certified) | Substantiation risk | Only use with current third-party certification on file. | "certified cruelty-free by [certifying body]" once verified |


### General Principles (for anything not explicitly listed)

1. **No unsubstantiated superlatives** ("best," "#1," "revolutionary") without a citable source on file with Legal.
2. **No absolute guarantees** of outcome, result, or performance -- individual results vary and must be described that way.
3. **No manufactured urgency** ("only hours left," "prices going up forever") unless the constraint is real and verifiable.
4. **No borrowed authority** (implying a certification, endorsement, or approval that hasn't been formally granted and documented).

## 6. Formatting Standards

| Standard | Rule |
|---|---|
| Capitalization | Sentence case for headlines, buttons, and subject lines (capitalize only the first word and proper nouns). Do not use Title Case or ALL CAPS except for the brand wordmark itself. |
| Oxford comma | Always used in lists of three or more items: "colors, fonts, and tone" -- not "colors, fonts and tone." |
| Numbers | Spell out one through nine in body copy; use numerals for 10 and above, all statistics, and all measurements (e.g. "3 steps," "nine reasons," "24/7"). |
| Dates | Write dates as Month D, YYYY (e.g. "July 2, 2026") in body copy; MM/DD/YYYY is permitted only in dense UI tables. |
| Emoji usage | Emoji are welcome in social and push copy (max 2 per post) to reinforce tone -- never in formal emails, legal copy, or disclosures. |
| Hashtags | Maximum 3 hashtags per social post, placed at the end of the caption, never inline in a sentence. The branded hashtag comes first. |
| Links & CTAs | Buttons and links use an active verb + outcome ("Start your plan," not "Click here" or "Learn more"). Never use a bare URL in body copy on channels that support link text. |
| Accessibility | Every image in customer-facing content must ship with descriptive alt text (not "image of..."); every video must include captions before publishing. |
| Acronyms | Spell out any acronym on first use per document/page, followed by the acronym in parentheses; the acronym alone may be used thereafter. |
| Contractions | Contractions are allowed and encouraged ("we're," "you'll") to keep the voice conversational, except inside formal disclosures and legal text. |

Character limits are hard constraints (Section 6 covers the full table); this section covers *how* to use each channel well within those constraints.

### Instagram

Lead visually; caption supports the image rather than repeating it. Best for brand-world content, product detail, and community-facing tone.

### TikTok

Fastest-moving channel -- voice can flex slightly more casual here than elsewhere, but restricted words and disclosures still apply in full.

**Example (on-brand):**
> VOICEOVER: 'At Auravue Beauty, we believe Tech-Forward Gen Z deserve better. That's why we help you make the smart choice without the sales pitch.'

### LinkedIn

Most formal social channel. Use for company milestones, hiring, thought leadership -- tone shifts toward "sophisticated" and "reassuring" pillars over "witty" or "edgy" ones.

**Example (on-brand):**
> (see brand voice section for tone reference)

### Email

Highest-trust, most personal channel. Subject line earns the open; body copy earns the click. Disclosures are never optional here.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Real talk — how Auravue Beauty helps you make the smart choice without the sales pitch

### SMS / Push

Shortest attention span, highest interruption cost. One idea per message, always.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Okay, this is exciting! Your Auravue Beauty update is ready — a quick way to make the smart choice without the sales pitch.

### Blog / SEO

Longest-form brand voice expression. This is where a voice pillar can be explained, not just demonstrated.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Designed for Tech-Forward Gen Z, this is Auravue Beauty doing what it does best — helping you make the smart choice without the sales pitch, without the noise.

### Paid Search / Display

Least room for personality -- clarity and compliance outrank cleverness. Every claim needs to survive legal review at 30 characters.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Auravue Beauty: Auravue — Do It Right.

### Customer Support

Voice pillars still apply, but empathy and clarity always outrank wit or edge, especially in complaints or errors.

**Example (on-brand):**
> (see brand voice section for tone reference)



### Channel-Specific Character Limits

| Channel | Field | Character Limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| X / Twitter | Post text | 280 characters | Leave room for a link (~23 chars) if sharing one. |
| Instagram | Caption | 2,200 characters (aim for first 125 visible) | Front-load the hook; caption truncates after ~3 lines. |
| Instagram | Bio | 150 characters | One line of value prop + one line of CTA max. |
| Facebook | Post text | 63,206 characters (aim for under 80 for engagement) | Short posts consistently outperform long ones in feed. |
| LinkedIn | Post text | 3,000 characters (aim for under 200 above the fold) | First 2-3 lines show before 'See more' -- put the point there. |
| TikTok | Caption | 2,200 characters (aim for under 150) | Hook must land in the first 3 words. |
| Email | Subject line | 60 characters (front-load first 30 for mobile) | Avoid ALL CAPS and more than one exclamation point. |
| Email | Preview text | 90 characters | Must not repeat the subject line verbatim. |
| SMS / Text | Message body | 160 characters (single segment) | Longer messages split into multiple segments and may be billed/delivered separately. |
| Push Notification | Title | 50 characters | Titles longer than this truncate on most Android/iOS lock screens. |
| Push Notification | Body | 150 characters | Lead with the benefit, not the feature. |
| SEO / Meta | Meta description | 155-160 characters | Google truncates beyond this; include the primary keyword early. |
| Paid Search | Headline (Google Ads) | 30 characters per headline | Up to 3 headlines are combined; each must stand alone. |
| Blog / Web | Page title / H1 | 60 characters | Keep the brand name out of the title unless it's the subject. |

## 8. Required Disclosures & Disclaimers

Auravue Beauty operates in a category with specific disclosure obligations. The table below defines exactly when a disclosure is required, the approved wording, and where it must appear. Do not paraphrase disclosure text without Legal sign-off.

| Content Scenario | Required Disclosure Text | Placement Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Any ingredient efficacy claim (e.g. "reduces redness") | "Individual results may vary." | Immediately after the claim, same visual weight as surrounding text. |
| Influencer or gifted-product posts | "#ad" or "#Auravuepartner" (per current FTC endorsement guidance) | In the first 3 lines of the caption, not buried in a hashtag block at the end. |
| Before/after imagery | "Unedited. Results after 4 weeks of consistent use." | Overlaid on or directly beneath the image, not just in the caption. |


**General disclaimer (use when no scenario above applies but a claim is being made):**
> "Individual results may vary."

### Regional Disclosure Requirements

| Region | Requirement |
|---|---|
| United States | FTC endorsement guidance applies to all gifted/sponsored content: disclose material connections clearly and conspicuously. |
| European Union | EU Cosmetics Regulation (EC) 1223/2009 applies: ingredient claims must match the product's Cosmetic Product Safety Report on file. |
| California (Prop 65) | Any product containing a listed substance above threshold requires a Prop 65 warning on packaging and product pages. |

## 9. Crisis & Sensitive-Topic Communication Protocol

**Acknowledge fast.** Publicly acknowledge any service disruption, error, or complaint pattern within 1 hour, even if the full fix isn't ready. Silence reads worse than an honest "we're on it."

**Drop the personality, keep the humanity.** Suspend Auravue Beauty's lighter tone pillars (wit, edge, playfulness) during an active incident. Empathy and clarity remain non-negotiable.

**One voice, one channel of record.** Designate a single channel (usually the status page or a pinned post) as the source of truth; all other channels link back to it rather than repeating varying details.

**No speculation.** Never guess at root cause, timeline, or blame publicly. State only what is confirmed.

**Close the loop.** Once resolved, publish a plain-language summary of what happened and what changed -- silence after resolution erodes trust as much as silence during the incident.

**Legal & Compliance sign-off.** Any incident involving data, safety, or regulated claims requires Compliance sign-off before any public statement, even a holding statement.
