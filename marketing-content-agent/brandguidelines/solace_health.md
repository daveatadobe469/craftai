## 1. About This Guidebook

**Purpose.** This guidebook defines how Solace Health sounds, what language is off-limits, how content must be formatted for each channel, and which legal disclosures are required before publishing. It applies to every employee, agency, freelancer, and AI tool producing customer-facing content on Solace Health's behalf.

**Mission.** Our mission is to bring honesty and clarity back to digital healthcare, so people feel informed rather than alarmed.

**Who this is for:** Marketing, Content, Social, Customer Support, Legal & Compliance review, and any external agency or contractor producing Solace Health content.

**Scope:** Applies to all channels -- social, email, SMS, push, web, paid media, and customer support -- across every market Solace Health operates in.

**How to use this document:** Sections 2-3 define *how we sound*. Sections 4-6 define *what's off-limits and how content must be formatted*. Section 7 is a channel-by-channel playbook. Sections 8-9 cover legal disclosures and crisis protocol. Appendices A and B are a machine-readable rule set and worked examples, useful for training reviewers (human or automated) on real compliant vs. non-compliant content.

## 2. Brand Foundations

### Brand Story

Solace Health started as a side project between two nurses frustrated by how confusing portal messages and lab results were for patients. The founding team's rule was simple: if a message would confuse your own mother, rewrite it. That rule still governs every piece of content the brand publishes.

### Positioning Statement

> For busy people who feel lost in their own healthcare, Solace Health is the digital health platform that translates medical complexity into plain, honest language -- unlike portals that just hand you a PDF and a phone number.

### Competitive Differentiation

Where most healthcare apps default to clinical hedging and legal-safe vagueness, Solace Health leads with candor: we tell people what we know, what we don't, and exactly what happens next. Warmth is not a tagline here -- it's a writing rule.

### Audience Personas


**Dana, 34** -- Marketing manager, first-time parent

- **Goals:** Get quick, trustworthy answers between meetings without a 45-minute portal hunt.
- **Frustrations:** Medical jargon, being told to 'consult her doctor' with no other detail.
- **Primary channels:** Push notifications, in-app messages, email


**Marcus, 61** -- Managing a chronic condition post-retirement

- **Goals:** Understand trends in his numbers over time without a data science degree.
- **Frustrations:** Tiny text, inconsistent terminology between visits.
- **Primary channels:** Email, printed after-visit summaries, phone support

## 3. Messaging Pillars & Tagline Library

### Messaging Pillars


**Clarity over comprehensiveness.** We'd rather say one true, useful thing clearly than ten hedged things vaguely.
> "Your cholesterol is a little high. Here's the one change that matters most right now."


**Partnership, not paternalism.** We talk with people about their health, not at them.
> "Let's figure out what's next together."


**Radical plain language.** Every clinical term is translated the first time it's used.
> "Your provider ordered an A1C (a 3-month blood sugar average)."

## 4. Brand Voice & Tone

Solace Health's voice is **empathetic, inclusive, candid, reassuring**. These traits should be recognizable in every piece of content, regardless of channel or author.


### Empathetic

We acknowledge that health concerns are personal and often stressful before we get to the facts.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "That sounds stressful -- here's what we can do together." | "Please see below for symptom protocol." |


### Candid

We say what we know and clearly flag what we don't, instead of hedging with vague reassurance.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "This result is within normal range, but a specialist should confirm." | "Nothing to worry about, probably!" |


### Inclusive

We write for people of all health literacy levels -- no jargon without a plain-language explanation.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Your A1C (a 3-month blood sugar average) is trending down." | "Your A1C is improving." (no explanation given) |


### Reassuring

We close every difficult message with a clear, calm next step -- never leave someone hanging.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Here's exactly what happens next, step by step." | "A provider will be in touch at some point." |



### Tone by Context

| Context | Tone Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Marketing & social | Full voice expression -- lead with personality within the pillars above. |
| Transactional (receipts, confirmations) | Warm but efficient; clarity outranks personality here. |
| Customer support (routine) | Calm, helpful, human -- avoid scripted-sounding phrases. |
| Customer support (complaints or errors) | Extra empathy, zero humor, always a clear next step. |
| Legal, disclosures, and compliance copy | Neutral and precise; voice pillars do not override legal accuracy. |

## 5. Restricted & Prohibited Language

The words and phrases below are banned in all Solace Health customer-facing content. If a word isn't listed here but feels like it violates the spirit of this list (hype, unsubstantiated claims, or off-tone language), escalate to Brand or Legal before publishing.

| Restricted Term | Category | Why It's Restricted | Use Instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| cure | Medical claim risk | Implies guaranteed medical outcomes we cannot promise. | "may help manage," "is designed to support" |
| guaranteed results | Medical claim risk | No health outcome can be guaranteed for an individual. | "many members report," "clinically studied to help" |
| FDA-approved (unverified) | Regulatory / legal risk | Only use FDA terminology if legal has verified current clearance status for that exact product. | "cleared for [specific use]" only after legal sign-off |
| best in the world | Unsubstantiated superlative | Not a verifiable or substantiated claim. | "one of the ways we help," or a specific, cited stat |
| cheap | Devalues the offering; also a banned brand-tone word | Positions healthcare as a discount commodity, which undermines trust. | "affordable," "accessible pricing" |
| diagnose / diagnosis (in marketing copy) | Scope-of-practice risk | Only licensed clinicians in a clinical encounter may diagnose; marketing copy must not imply the product diagnoses. | "helps you understand your symptoms," "screening tool" |
| miracle | Medical claim risk | No treatment is a miracle; this is a classic FTC/FDA red flag phrase. | "a treatment option that's helped many members" |
| 100% safe | Unsubstantiated safety claim | No medical product or service can claim zero risk. | "generally well-tolerated," "reviewed by our clinical team" |
| instant relief | Unsubstantiated claim | Implies an immediate guaranteed outcome we can't promise. | "fast-acting," with timeframe data if available |
| painless | Unsubstantiated claim | Pain tolerance varies; absolute claims invite complaints and legal risk. | "designed to minimize discomfort" |
| addiction-free / non-addictive (unqualified) | Regulatory risk | Requires clinical substantiation on file before use. | cite the specific study or omit the claim |
| doctor recommended (unqualified) | Substantiation risk | Must name the specific study, panel, or number of doctors surveyed. | "recommended by our clinical advisory board of licensed physicians" |
| risk-free trial | Regulatory / brand-tone risk | Combines two red-flag terms; healthcare trials still carry inherent risk. | "free 14-day trial, cancel anytime" |
| all-natural (unqualified) | FTC substantiation risk | Requires ingredient-level substantiation. | list the specific ingredients instead |


### General Principles (for anything not explicitly listed)

1. **No unsubstantiated superlatives** ("best," "#1," "revolutionary") without a citable source on file with Legal.
2. **No absolute guarantees** of outcome, result, or performance -- individual results vary and must be described that way.
3. **No manufactured urgency** ("only hours left," "prices going up forever") unless the constraint is real and verifiable.
4. **No borrowed authority** (implying a certification, endorsement, or approval that hasn't been formally granted and documented).

## 6. Formatting Standards

| Standard | Rule |
|---|---|
| Capitalization | Sentence case for headlines, buttons, and subject lines (capitalize only the first word and proper nouns). Do not use Title Case or ALL CAPS except for the brand wordmark itself. |
| Oxford comma | Always used in lists of three or more items: "colors, fonts, and tone" -- not "colors, fonts and tone." |
| Numbers | Spell out one through nine in body copy; use numerals for 10 and above, all statistics, and all measurements (e.g. "3 steps," "nine reasons," "24/7"). |
| Dates | Write dates as Month D, YYYY (e.g. "July 2, 2026") in body copy; MM/DD/YYYY is permitted only in dense UI tables. |
| Emoji usage | Emoji are not used in any Solace Health content, including social. The brand voice communicates warmth and energy through word choice, not iconography. |
| Hashtags | Maximum 3 hashtags per social post, placed at the end of the caption, never inline in a sentence. The branded hashtag comes first. |
| Links & CTAs | Buttons and links use an active verb + outcome ("Start your plan," not "Click here" or "Learn more"). Never use a bare URL in body copy on channels that support link text. |
| Accessibility | Every image in customer-facing content must ship with descriptive alt text (not "image of..."); every video must include captions before publishing. |
| Acronyms | Spell out any acronym on first use per document/page, followed by the acronym in parentheses; the acronym alone may be used thereafter. |
| Contractions | Contractions are allowed and encouraged ("we're," "you'll") to keep the voice conversational, except inside formal disclosures and legal text. |

### Localization Notes

- Translated content must be reviewed by a native-speaking editor, not machine-translated and published directly.
- Idioms and wordplay in the Clash Display-styled headline system rarely translate -- prioritize meaning over literal wording when localizing.
- Currency, date, and measurement formats follow local convention (e.g. DD/MM/YYYY and metric units outside the US) even when the surrounding copy is in English for a US-based CMS.

## 7. Channel Playbook

Character limits are hard constraints (Section 6 covers the full table); this section covers *how* to use each channel well within those constraints.

### Instagram

Lead visually; caption supports the image rather than repeating it. Best for brand-world content, product detail, and community-facing tone.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Hey — meet the Solace Health way to get more done with less hassle. Solace — Do It Right. #Solace

### TikTok

Fastest-moving channel -- voice can flex slightly more casual here than elsewhere, but restricted words and disclosures still apply in full.

**Example (on-brand):**
> VOICEOVER: 'At Solace Health, we believe Urban Commuters deserve better. That's why we help you get more done with less hassle.'

### LinkedIn

Most formal social channel. Use for company milestones, hiring, thought leadership -- tone shifts toward "sophisticated" and "reassuring" pillars over "witty" or "edgy" ones.

**Example (on-brand):**
> (see brand voice section for tone reference)

### Email

Highest-trust, most personal channel. Subject line earns the open; body copy earns the click. Disclosures are never optional here.

**Example (on-brand):**
> (see brand voice section for tone reference)

### SMS / Push

Shortest attention span, highest interruption cost. One idea per message, always.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Hey! Your Solace Health update is ready — a quick way to get more done with less hassle.

### Blog / SEO

Longest-form brand voice expression. This is where a voice pillar can be explained, not just demonstrated.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Designed for Urban Commuters, this is Solace Health doing what it does best — helping you get more done with less hassle, without the noise.

### Paid Search / Display

Least room for personality -- clarity and compliance outrank cleverness. Every claim needs to survive legal review at 30 characters.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Solace Health: Solace — Do It Right.

### Customer Support

Voice pillars still apply, but empathy and clarity always outrank wit or edge, especially in complaints or errors.

**Example (on-brand):**
> (see brand voice section for tone reference)



### Channel-Specific Character Limits

| Channel | Field | Character Limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| X / Twitter | Post text | 280 characters | Leave room for a link (~23 chars) if sharing one. |
| Instagram | Caption | 2,200 characters (aim for first 125 visible) | Front-load the hook; caption truncates after ~3 lines. |
| Instagram | Bio | 150 characters | One line of value prop + one line of CTA max. |
| Facebook | Post text | 63,206 characters (aim for under 80 for engagement) | Short posts consistently outperform long ones in feed. |
| LinkedIn | Post text | 3,000 characters (aim for under 200 above the fold) | First 2-3 lines show before 'See more' -- put the point there. |
| TikTok | Caption | 2,200 characters (aim for under 150) | Hook must land in the first 3 words. |
| Email | Subject line | 60 characters (front-load first 30 for mobile) | Avoid ALL CAPS and more than one exclamation point. |
| Email | Preview text | 90 characters | Must not repeat the subject line verbatim. |
| SMS / Text | Message body | 160 characters (single segment) | Longer messages split into multiple segments and may be billed/delivered separately. |
| Push Notification | Title | 50 characters | Titles longer than this truncate on most Android/iOS lock screens. |
| Push Notification | Body | 150 characters | Lead with the benefit, not the feature. |
| SEO / Meta | Meta description | 155-160 characters | Google truncates beyond this; include the primary keyword early. |
| Paid Search | Headline (Google Ads) | 30 characters per headline | Up to 3 headlines are combined; each must stand alone. |
| Blog / Web | Page title / H1 | 60 characters | Keep the brand name out of the title unless it's the subject. |

## 8. Required Disclosures & Disclaimers

Solace Health operates in a category with specific disclosure obligations. The table below defines exactly when a disclosure is required, the approved wording, and where it must appear. Do not paraphrase disclosure text without Legal sign-off.

| Content Scenario | Required Disclosure Text | Placement Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Any content describing a health benefit, outcome, or symptom relief | "Results may vary; not a substitute for professional advice." | Immediately below the claim, same font size no smaller than 70% of body text. |
| Testimonials or member success stories | "[Name]'s experience is individual and results are not guaranteed for all members." | Directly beneath the testimonial, never in a footnote only. |
| Any content mentioning a specific condition, medication, or treatment | "This is not medical advice. Consult a licensed provider before making changes to your care." | At the end of the post/article, above the CTA. |
| Paid partnerships or sponsored health content | "Sponsored content. Solace Health received compensation for this placement." | At the very top of the content, before any claims are made (FTC requirement). |


**General disclaimer (use when no scenario above applies but a claim is being made):**
> "Results may vary; not a substitute for professional advice."

### Regional Disclosure Requirements

| Region | Requirement |
|---|---|
| United States | HIPAA notice required on any page collecting health information: "Your information is protected under HIPAA. See our Notice of Privacy Practices." |
| European Union / UK | GDPR notice required: "Your health data is processed under GDPR Article 9 special category protections. See our Data Processing Notice." |
| California | CCPA notice required on data-collection forms: "California residents have the right to know, delete, and opt out of the sale of personal information." |

## 9. Crisis & Sensitive-Topic Communication Protocol

**Acknowledge fast.** Publicly acknowledge any service disruption, error, or complaint pattern within 1 hour, even if the full fix isn't ready. Silence reads worse than an honest "we're on it."

**Drop the personality, keep the humanity.** Suspend Solace Health's lighter tone pillars (wit, edge, playfulness) during an active incident. Empathy and clarity remain non-negotiable.

**One voice, one channel of record.** Designate a single channel (usually the status page or a pinned post) as the source of truth; all other channels link back to it rather than repeating varying details.

**No speculation.** Never guess at root cause, timeline, or blame publicly. State only what is confirmed.

**Close the loop.** Once resolved, publish a plain-language summary of what happened and what changed -- silence after resolution erodes trust as much as silence during the incident.

**Legal & Compliance sign-off.** Any incident involving data, safety, or regulated claims requires Compliance sign-off before any public statement, even a holding statement.
