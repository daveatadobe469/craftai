## 1. About This Guidebook

**Purpose.** This guidebook defines how Sable & Finch sounds, what language is off-limits, how content must be formatted for each channel, and which legal disclosures are required before publishing. It applies to every employee, agency, freelancer, and AI tool producing customer-facing content on Sable & Finch's behalf.

**Mission.** Our mission is to give considered dressers the tools to build a wardrobe of lasting pieces instead of chasing seasonal trends.

**Who this is for:** Marketing, Content, Social, Customer Support, Legal & Compliance review, and any external agency or contractor producing Sable & Finch content.

**Scope:** Applies to all channels -- social, email, SMS, push, web, paid media, and customer support -- across every market Sable & Finch operates in.

**How to use this document:** Sections 2-3 define *how we sound*. Sections 4-6 define *what's off-limits and how content must be formatted*. Section 7 is a channel-by-channel playbook. Sections 8-9 cover legal disclosures and crisis protocol. Appendices A and B are a machine-readable rule set and worked examples, useful for training reviewers (human or automated) on real compliant vs. non-compliant content.

## 2. Brand Foundations

### Brand Story

Sable & Finch was founded by two pattern-makers who left fast-fashion houses within a year of each other, both for the same reason: watching perfectly good garments engineered to fall apart on schedule. The brand's entire visual and verbal identity is built around a single idea -- make it once, make it right, say only what's true about it.

### Positioning Statement

> For discerning buyers building a wardrobe of lasting pieces, Sable & Finch is the label that lets craftsmanship speak for itself -- unlike luxury marketing that substitutes hype adjectives for actual material and construction detail.

### Competitive Differentiation

Where much of luxury fashion marketing relies on aspirational imagery and vague superlatives, Sable & Finch describes the actual denim weight, the actual stitch count, the actual sourcing -- letting precision read as confidence.

### Audience Personas


**Adrienne, 44** -- Creative director, wardrobe rebuild after years of fast fashion

- **Goals:** Buy fewer, better pieces and understand exactly what she's paying for.
- **Frustrations:** Luxury marketing copy that says nothing concrete about construction or materials.
- **Primary channels:** Email, editorial/press, in-store


**Wes, 38** -- Architect, minimalist wardrobe philosophy

- **Goals:** Pieces that photograph as well in year five as year one.
- **Frustrations:** Brands that quietly reduce quality while raising prices.
- **Primary channels:** Website, email, print lookbook

## 3. Messaging Pillars & Tagline Library

### Messaging Pillars


**Precision over hype.** Specific material and construction facts replace vague superlatives.
> "14oz cotton canvas, triple-stitched at every stress point."


**Restraint as confidence.** We let quality imply value rather than announcing it.
> "A coat built for the next decade, not the next season."


**Craft made visible.** We show the process, not just the finished product.
> "Each bag is hand-stitched by one of six artisans -- their initials are stamped inside."

## 4. Brand Voice & Tone

Sable & Finch's voice is **sophisticated, reassuring, precise, understated**. These traits should be recognizable in every piece of content, regardless of channel or author.


### Sophisticated

We write the way our garments are cut: precise, considered, nothing extraneous.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Full-grain leather. Hand-stitched. Built to outlast the season." | "OMG this bag is EVERYTHING!!" |


### Reassuring

Confidence without needing to shout -- the product speaks first.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Every piece is inspected by hand before it ships." | "You won't believe how amazing this quality is!!!" |


### Precise

Specific material and construction facts replace vague superlatives.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "12oz Japanese selvedge denim, sanforized for minimal shrinkage." | "The best denim you'll ever own." |


### Understated

Restraint is the brand's confidence signal -- we let quality imply value, not adjectives.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "A coat for the next decade, not the next season." | "This coat will change your life!" |



### Tone by Context

| Context | Tone Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Marketing & social | Full voice expression -- lead with personality within the pillars above. |
| Transactional (receipts, confirmations) | Warm but efficient; clarity outranks personality here. |
| Customer support (routine) | Calm, helpful, human -- avoid scripted-sounding phrases. |
| Customer support (complaints or errors) | Extra empathy, zero humor, always a clear next step. |
| Legal, disclosures, and compliance copy | Neutral and precise; voice pillars do not override legal accuracy. |

## 5. Restricted & Prohibited Language

The words and phrases below are banned in all Sable & Finch customer-facing content. If a word isn't listed here but feels like it violates the spirit of this list (hype, unsubstantiated claims, or off-tone language), escalate to Brand or Legal before publishing.

| Restricted Term | Category | Why It's Restricted | Use Instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| guaranteed | Brand-tone violation | Absolute claims read as insecure, not confident, within a luxury register. | "crafted to last," "built for years of wear" |
| cheap | Brand-tone violation | Directly contradicts the brand's premium/craftsmanship positioning. | "considered pricing for the craftsmanship involved" |
| revolutionary (overused) | Diluted superlative | Luxury copy earns its claims through specificity, not hype adjectives. | name the specific material/construction detail instead |
| hack | Register mismatch | Casual internet slang breaks the sophisticated, understated tone. | "a simpler way to style it" |
| risk-free | Brand-tone violation / vague | Vague hedge language undercuts the confident, precise voice. | state the specific return/exchange policy instead |
| luxurious (unqualified) | Brand-tone violation / vague | Vague adjective the reader can't verify; replace with the specific material fact. | name the specific material/construction detail |
| exclusive (unless literally limited) | Substantiation / brand-tone risk | Only use when quantity or access is genuinely limited and documented. | state the actual production run size |
| finest (unqualified) | Vague superlative | Unverifiable claim inconsistent with the brand's precision-over-hype pillar. | cite the specific material grade or origin |
| timeless (overused) | Diluted superlative | Overused in luxury marketing to the point of meaning nothing. | describe the specific design choice that ages well |
| investment piece (unqualified) | Vague / unsubstantiated framing | Implies a financial claim without evidence. | describe expected longevity based on construction |


### General Principles (for anything not explicitly listed)

1. **No unsubstantiated superlatives** ("best," "#1," "revolutionary") without a citable source on file with Legal.
2. **No absolute guarantees** of outcome, result, or performance -- individual results vary and must be described that way.
3. **No manufactured urgency** ("only hours left," "prices going up forever") unless the constraint is real and verifiable.
4. **No borrowed authority** (implying a certification, endorsement, or approval that hasn't been formally granted and documented).

## 6. Formatting Standards

| Standard | Rule |
|---|---|
| Capitalization | Sentence case for headlines, buttons, and subject lines (capitalize only the first word and proper nouns). Do not use Title Case or ALL CAPS except for the brand wordmark itself. |
| Oxford comma | Always used in lists of three or more items: "colors, fonts, and tone" -- not "colors, fonts and tone." |
| Numbers | Spell out one through nine in body copy; use numerals for 10 and above, all statistics, and all measurements (e.g. "3 steps," "nine reasons," "24/7"). |
| Dates | Write dates as Month D, YYYY (e.g. "July 2, 2026") in body copy; MM/DD/YYYY is permitted only in dense UI tables. |
| Emoji usage | Emoji are not used in any Sable & Finch content, including social. The brand voice communicates warmth and energy through word choice, not iconography. |
| Hashtags | Maximum 3 hashtags per social post, placed at the end of the caption, never inline in a sentence. The branded hashtag comes first. |
| Links & CTAs | Buttons and links use an active verb + outcome ("Start your plan," not "Click here" or "Learn more"). Never use a bare URL in body copy on channels that support link text. |
| Accessibility | Every image in customer-facing content must ship with descriptive alt text (not "image of..."); every video must include captions before publishing. |
| Acronyms | Spell out any acronym on first use per document/page, followed by the acronym in parentheses; the acronym alone may be used thereafter. |
| Contractions | Contractions are allowed and encouraged ("we're," "you'll") to keep the voice conversational, except inside formal disclosures and legal text. |

### Localization Notes

- Translated content must be reviewed by a native-speaking editor, not machine-translated and published directly.
- Idioms and wordplay in the Bebas Neue-styled headline system rarely translate -- prioritize meaning over literal wording when localizing.
- Currency, date, and measurement formats follow local convention (e.g. DD/MM/YYYY and metric units outside the US) even when the surrounding copy is in English for a US-based CMS.

## 7. Channel Playbook

Character limits are hard constraints (Section 6 covers the full table); this section covers *how* to use each channel well within those constraints.

### Instagram

Lead visually; caption supports the image rather than repeating it. Best for brand-world content, product detail, and community-facing tone.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Real talk — meet the Sable & Finch way to get more done with less hassle. Sable — Do It Right. #Sable

### TikTok

Fastest-moving channel -- voice can flex slightly more casual here than elsewhere, but restricted words and disclosures still apply in full.

**Example (on-brand):**
> VOICEOVER: 'At Sable & Finch, we believe Growing Enterprises deserve better. That's why we help you get more done with less hassle.'

### LinkedIn

Most formal social channel. Use for company milestones, hiring, thought leadership -- tone shifts toward "sophisticated" and "reassuring" pillars over "witty" or "edgy" ones.

**Example (on-brand):**
> (see brand voice section for tone reference)

### Email

Highest-trust, most personal channel. Subject line earns the open; body copy earns the click. Disclosures are never optional here.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Quick one for you — how Sable & Finch helps you get more done with less hassle

### SMS / Push

Shortest attention span, highest interruption cost. One idea per message, always.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Let's be honest! Your Sable & Finch update is ready — a quick way to get more done with less hassle.

### Blog / SEO

Longest-form brand voice expression. This is where a voice pillar can be explained, not just demonstrated.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Sable & Finch today reaffirmed its mission to give growing enterprises the tools to move faster without cutting corners, announcing a new initiative aimed at helping Growing Enterprises get more done with less hassle.

### Paid Search / Display

Least room for personality -- clarity and compliance outrank cleverness. Every claim needs to survive legal review at 30 characters.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Sable & Finch: Sable — Do It Right.

### Customer Support

Voice pillars still apply, but empathy and clarity always outrank wit or edge, especially in complaints or errors.

**Example (on-brand):**
> Thanks for reaching out! We hear you, and we're on it — Sable & Finch's here to make sure you get more done with less hassle. We'll follow up within one business day.



### Channel-Specific Character Limits

| Channel | Field | Character Limit | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| X / Twitter | Post text | 280 characters | Leave room for a link (~23 chars) if sharing one. |
| Instagram | Caption | 2,200 characters (aim for first 125 visible) | Front-load the hook; caption truncates after ~3 lines. |
| Instagram | Bio | 150 characters | One line of value prop + one line of CTA max. |
| Facebook | Post text | 63,206 characters (aim for under 80 for engagement) | Short posts consistently outperform long ones in feed. |
| LinkedIn | Post text | 3,000 characters (aim for under 200 above the fold) | First 2-3 lines show before 'See more' -- put the point there. |
| TikTok | Caption | 2,200 characters (aim for under 150) | Hook must land in the first 3 words. |
| Email | Subject line | 60 characters (front-load first 30 for mobile) | Avoid ALL CAPS and more than one exclamation point. |
| Email | Preview text | 90 characters | Must not repeat the subject line verbatim. |
| SMS / Text | Message body | 160 characters (single segment) | Longer messages split into multiple segments and may be billed/delivered separately. |
| Push Notification | Title | 50 characters | Titles longer than this truncate on most Android/iOS lock screens. |
| Push Notification | Body | 150 characters | Lead with the benefit, not the feature. |
| SEO / Meta | Meta description | 155-160 characters | Google truncates beyond this; include the primary keyword early. |
| Paid Search | Headline (Google Ads) | 30 characters per headline | Up to 3 headlines are combined; each must stand alone. |
| Blog / Web | Page title / H1 | 60 characters | Keep the brand name out of the title unless it's the subject. |

## 8. Required Disclosures & Disclaimers

Sable & Finch operates in a category with specific disclosure obligations. The table below defines exactly when a disclosure is required, the approved wording, and where it must appear. Do not paraphrase disclosure text without Legal sign-off.

| Content Scenario | Required Disclosure Text | Placement Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Limited-edition or made-to-order product claims | "Availability is limited and not guaranteed until order confirmation." | Directly beneath the product description. |
| Sustainability or material-sourcing claims | "Sourcing details reflect current supplier documentation on file; contact us for full material provenance." | At the end of the product page copy. |


**General disclaimer (use when no scenario above applies but a claim is being made):**
> "Availability is limited and not guaranteed until order confirmation."

## 9. Crisis & Sensitive-Topic Communication Protocol

**Acknowledge fast.** Publicly acknowledge any service disruption, error, or complaint pattern within 1 hour, even if the full fix isn't ready. Silence reads worse than an honest "we're on it."

**Drop the personality, keep the humanity.** Suspend Sable & Finch's lighter tone pillars (wit, edge, playfulness) during an active incident. Empathy and clarity remain non-negotiable.

**One voice, one channel of record.** Designate a single channel (usually the status page or a pinned post) as the source of truth; all other channels link back to it rather than repeating varying details.

**No speculation.** Never guess at root cause, timeline, or blame publicly. State only what is confirmed.

**Close the loop.** Once resolved, publish a plain-language summary of what happened and what changed -- silence after resolution erodes trust as much as silence during the incident.

**Legal & Compliance sign-off.** Any incident involving data, safety, or regulated claims requires Compliance sign-off before any public statement, even a holding statement.
